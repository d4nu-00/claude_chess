# Opening-family plans

Each section: `## <family>` then `match:` (comma-separated name prefixes from the lichess
ECO names) and 1-2 lines of typical plans. The context builder matches the identified
opening name against `match:` prefixes (longest prefix wins).

## Ruy Lopez
match: Ruy Lopez
White: slow pressure on e5, c3+d4 centre, Nbd2-f1-g3 manoeuvre, a4 vs ...b5. Black: ...b5/...Na5 queenside space, ...d5 or ...c5 counterplay; Berlin endgame after Qxd8 — Black has bishop pair, White kingside majority.

## Italian Game
match: Italian Game, Giuoco Piano, Two Knights
White: c3+d3 slow build (a4, Re1, Nbd2-f1-g3) or c3-d4 central break; watch f7. Black: ...d6/...a6, ...Be6 to trade the c4 bishop, ...d5 break when ready.

## Scotch and open e-games
match: Scotch Game, Four Knights Game, Center Game, Ponziani Opening, Scotch Gambit
Open centre with fast development; White uses e4 space and Nd4, Black strikes with ...d5 and piece activity. Watch pins and forks on the e-file.

## Petrov
match: Petrov's Defense, Russian Game
Symmetrical and solid; White tries to exploit slight lead in development (c4, Nc3, O-O, Re1 pressure on e-file). Black equalises by ...d5, ...Bd6, ...O-O, keeping the Ne4 supported.

## King's Gambit
match: King's Gambit
White gives f-pawn for rapid development and the half-open f-file (Bc4, O-O, attack on f7). Black: hold the pawn with ...g5 or return it with ...d5 for free development.

## Sicilian (Open)
match: Sicilian Defense
White: Nd4 centre, often opposite castling with g4/f4-f5 pawn storm or Bg5/Bc4 attacks on e6/f7. Black: half-open c-file, ...b5-b4 queenside counterplay, ...d5 break, exchange sac on c3.

## French
match: French Defense
Black: undermine White's d4-e5 chain with ...c5 and ...f6; bad light-squared bishop is the long-term issue. White: kingside space and attack (Qg4, f4-f5), maintain d4.

## Caro-Kann
match: Caro-Kann Defense
Black: solid structure, light-squared bishop developed outside the chain (...Bf5/...Bg4) before ...e6; ...c5 break. White: space (e5 advance) or piece activity; in Panov lines play with the IQP.

## Scandinavian and Alekhine
match: Scandinavian Defense, Alekhine Defense, Pirc Defense, Modern Defense
Black concedes centre to attack it later with pieces and ...c5/...e5. White: build a broad centre and develop quickly, avoid overextension.

## Queen's Gambit Declined
match: Queen's Gambit Declined, Tarrasch Defense
White: pressure on d5 and c-file, minority attack in Carlsbad (b4-b5), or central e4. Black: free the game with ...c5 or ...e5, develop the c8 bishop (…b6/…Bb7), Tarrasch accepts an IQP for activity.

## Queen's Gambit Accepted / Slav
match: Queen's Gambit Accepted, Slav Defense, Semi-Slav Defense, Queen's Gambit
Black: ...c5/...e5 central breaks; in Slav develop ...Bf5 before ...e6. White: big centre with e4, regain c4, sharp Meran/Botvinnik lines demand concrete tactics.

## Catalan
match: Catalan Opening
White: long-diagonal pressure with Bg2 on the b7/a8 squares, regain c4 pawn, queenside space. Black: complete development with ...c5 or ...b6/...Bb7; don't cling to the c4 pawn.

## Nimzo-Indian / Queen's Indian / Bogo
match: Nimzo-Indian Defense, Queen's Indian Defense, Bogo-Indian Defense
Black fights for e4 with pieces (...Bb4, ...b6/...Bb7, ...Ne4). Nimzo: Black often gives the bishop pair for doubled c-pawns — blockade and attack them; White: open the position for bishops with f3/e4.

## King's Indian
match: King's Indian Defense, Old Indian Defense, King's Indian Attack
Black: fianchetto, then ...e5 or ...c5 to hit d4; if centre locks (d5), ...f5-f4 kingside storm. White: big centre, queenside expansion c5 in locked lines, Be3/Qd2/h4 in Sämisch.

## Grünfeld
match: Grünfeld Defense, Neo-Grünfeld Defense
Black lets White build c3/d4/e4 then attacks it with ...c5, ...Bg7, ...Nc6, ...Qa5/…Bg4. White: keep the centre, d5 push, or passed d-pawn in endgames.

## Benoni and Benko
match: Benoni Defense, Benko Gambit, Czech Benoni
Black: queenside expansion (...b5, Benko's open a/b-files), long diagonal, ...Ne5. White: e4-e5 central break, Nc4 pressure on d6, restrain ...b5.

## Dutch
match: Dutch Defense
Black: control e4 with ...f5, kingside attack (...Qe8-h5) or Stonewall setup (d5/e6/f5). White: exploit e6/e5 holes, e4 break, fianchetto Bg2 against f5.

## English and Réti
match: English Opening, Réti Opening, Zukertort Opening
Flexible flank openings: White controls d5 from the side (c4, Nc3, Bg2), often a reversed Sicilian; queenside play with b4. Black: take the centre with ...e5/...d5 and develop harmoniously.

## London / Queen's Pawn systems
match: Queen's Pawn Game, London System, Colle System, Torre Attack, Trompowsky Attack
White: solid d4/e3/c3 pyramid, Bf4/Bg5 outside the chain, Ne5 + kingside attack or e4 break. Black: ...c5 and ...Qb6 hitting b2, or ...e5 break; don't allow a free Ne5 outpost.

## Flank and irregular
match: Bird Opening, Polish Opening, Nimzo-Larsen Attack, Hungarian Opening, Van Geet Opening, Grob Opening, Amar Opening
Unusual first moves: occupy the centre with pawns, develop normally, and punish loosening moves (early g4/b4/f4) with central breaks.
